Setup & tools

One-off checks and measurements. You will not need these often — which is exactly why they live here rather than in the way.

Reed Sweep

Walk up the keyboard one note at a time, holding each until Campfire takes the reading. It measures two things per reed: how far off pitch it sits, and how long it takes to speak. Then it tells you which reeds are the outliers.

Use a single-reed register (one dot, usually the 8′ "clarinet"). On a musette register two reeds beat against each other and the pitch you'd be reading is the average of a pair — a number neither reed has.

—
Tremolo Meter

Musette is two 8′ reeds tuned slightly apart. They beat against each other, and the beat rate is the tuning. Campfire reads the beat straight off the volume envelope and converts it to cents.

Switch to a musette register (two 8′ reeds — often marked with two dots side by side). Hold one note in the middle of the keyboard, steady, for the full six seconds.

Tuning Lab

Pick a target tuning — the needle guides every string toward it.

New strings drift — stretch them gently and retune a few times. Temperature changes and capos knock tuning out too; a 30-second check before practice is normal.

Capo Check

Clamp the capo just behind the fret, square across the neck. Then pick each string once, thickest first.

Capo Compass

A capo is a transposition machine. Campfire names a key and a shape family — you work out which fret gets you there, clamp it on, and pick each string to prove it.

—
Fret-12 Intonation Test

The 12th fret should sound exactly one octave above the open string. Pick a string, then follow the two steps. Fret lightly, fingertip just behind the fret.

A string that reads sharp at fret 12 no matter what is an instrument setup issue (saddle position) — a guitar tech's five-minute fix, not your fingers' fault.

Your instrument

Accordions differ from maker to maker in ways no chart can settle. Campfire needs a few facts about yours. Answer what you know; everything measurable is measured on the next two screens rather than typed here.

What Campfire measured

Blank rows mean "not measured yet" — never a guess. Run Register Check and the Stradella Mapper to fill them in.

Register Check

A register switch adds or removes whole reed banks, so the same key can sound an octave lower or higher than written. Hold one note and Campfire compares the energy an octave below, at pitch, and an octave above — telling you which ranks this switch actually engages.

Play and hold a note in the middle of the keyboard. Campfire needs about two seconds of steady tone.

—
Stradella Mapper

Two facts decide every chord Campfire can check, and both vary by maker: whether your 7th row keeps the root or drops it, and where the single chord reed bank starts. Neither can be looked up. This measures them.

You'll press ten buttons, one at a time, holding each until it's read. No time pressure — a long, steady press is exactly what makes this reliable.

Note Hunt

20 questions. Find the named note on the named string — the app hears your answer. Uses your current tuning.

Fret Sprint

One string, 60 seconds, as many found notes as you can. Frets 0–12. Wrong notes don't advance — accuracy is speed.

Scale Runner

Play the shape one note at a time — Campfire follows along and flags a wrong note the moment it hears one. Free tempo: accuracy first, speed later.

Drone Room

Same seven notes, different home. Campfire holds a root; you improvise over it. Every note you play is named live — chord tone, in-mode, the characteristic tone, or outside.

Earbuds strongly recommended — otherwise the mic hears the drone. Campfire only counts notes that arrive with a fresh pluck, so it ignores its own sustained tone.

Mode Detective

Campfire plays a phrase over a drone. You answer the only way that counts — by playing the one note that gives the mode away. Eight rounds.

The characteristic tone is the single note that separates a mode from plain major or minor. Find it and you've named the mode.

—
Interval Echo

Campfire plays two notes. You play the second one back. Ten rounds — the drill that turns theory into an ear.

Earbuds help — otherwise the mic hears Campfire's own notes. It waits for silence before listening either way.

—
Melodic Echo

A short phrase, played back by ear. Campfire plays three to five notes from one scale shape; you find them on the neck.

—
Quality Call

Campfire plays a chord; you answer with the single note that names it — the 3rd for major, the ♭3 for minor, the ♭5 for diminished. The third is where a chord's mood lives.

—
Bass Detective

Campfire plays a four-chord loop. You play back its root movement — just the bass line. Land it and the progression names itself.

—
Sing Back

Campfire plays a note; you sing it. The same detector reads a voice as easily as a string — and you can't sing a pitch you can't truly hear, which is why this builds an ear faster than anything else here.

Octaves don't matter — sing it wherever it sits comfortably. Tolerance is a generous ±30 cents, nothing here gates your progress, and you can switch it off again in About.

Chord Check

Arpeggio first — pick each string on its own and Campfire verifies every note, so it can tell you which finger is off. Pass that, and Strum mode judges the whole chord.

—
Change Counter

Sixty seconds, two chords, back and forth. Each verified change counts. This is the classic one-minute-changes drill — the fastest way to make chord shapes automatic.

Shape Shifter

The CAGED idea: five shapes you already know, slid anywhere on the neck. Campfire names a chord — you work out where the shape lands and play it.

—
Circle Runner

Your left hand is a circle of fifths laid out in a straight line. One column left is a fifth down, one column right is a fifth up — and that single fact is most of what makes the Stradella layout learnable. Campfire names a chord; you find the column.

Feel for the dimple on the C bass button and count from there. The buttons face away from you — nobody sees them, everybody feels them.

—
Target Practice

Campfire loops a progression and plays each chord as it arrives. Your job: land a chord tone in every bar. Hitting the 3rd or 7th — the notes that actually spell the harmony — scores double.

Tempo 70 BPM

Earbuds recommended. One note per bar is plenty — this is about choosing the right note, not playing many.

About Campfire

A guitar practice app that listens — built from classical signal processing, not AI. Everything runs on this phone: no accounts, no servers, no tracking, no network requests at all. Your audio is analysed live and never recorded or sent anywhere.
